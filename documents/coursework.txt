Description: Graduate-level course covering the theoretical foundations and applied methods of linear statistical modeling. Covers simple and multiple linear regression, model diagnostics, variable selection, regularization, and generalized linear models including logistic regression.

--- 2025 SPRING ---

DS 6001 | Practice and Application of Data Science | Grade: A
Description: Covers data science practice including communication, exploratory data analysis, and visualization. Selection of algorithms to suit the problem, user needs, and data type. Case studies explore the impact of data science across different domains. Bridges theoretical knowledge and real-world application.
Key topics: EDA, data visualization, algorithm selection, communication of results, case studies, interdisciplinary applications.

DS 6030 | Statistical Learning | Grade: A+
Description: Covers fundamentals of data mining and machine learning. Topics include regularization, classification, resampling methods, tree-based methods, ensemble methods, support vector machines, clustering, and dimensionality reduction. Emphasizes both theory and practical application using Python and R.
Key topics: Regression, classification, resampling, regularization (ridge/lasso), decision trees, random forests, gradient boosting, SVMs, unsupervised learning, model selection, bias-variance tradeoff.

--- 2025 SUMMER ---

DS 5012 | Computation for Data Science | Grade: A
Description: Covers numerical analysis, optimization, and distributed computing for data science applications. Topics include numerical errors, root-finding algorithms, numerical linear algebra, and large-scale data processing frameworks including Spark, resilient distributed datasets, and cloud computing tools.
Key topics: Numerical methods, optimization, numerical linear algebra, Spark, distributed computing, AWS, data storage and retrieval at scale.

DS 6040 | Bayesian Machine Learning | Grade: A
Description: Bayesian inferential methods provide a foundation for machine learning under conditions of uncertainty. Covers major Bayesian ML techniques to effectively address limits to understanding of complex world problems.
Key topics: Bayesian inference, conjugate prior probabilities, naive Bayes classifiers, expectation maximization (EM), Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), variational inference, Gaussian processes, probabilistic graphical models.

--- 2025 FALL ---

DS 5004 | Applied Reinforcement Learning | Grade: A
Description: Graduate course covering reinforcement learning theory and application. Students build and compare multiple RL architectures applied to real-world problems. Cameron applied this course directly to a traffic light optimization project comparing Tabular Q-Learning, Deep Q-Networks (DQN), and Policy Gradient methods.
Key topics: Markov decision processes, Q-learning, deep Q-networks (DQN), policy gradient methods, reward shaping, temporal difference learning, model-based and model-free RL, exploration vs. exploitation.

DS 6050 | ML III: Deep Learning | Grade: A-
Description: Graduate-level course on deep learning fundamentals and applications with emphasis on broad applicability across disciplines. Students complete substantive programming assignments implementing core architectures.
Key topics: Deep neural networks, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), LSTMs, transformers, encoder/decoder architectures, generative networks, GANs, regularization, optimization, sequence modeling.

--- 2026 SPRING ---

DS 5001 | Text as Data (Exploratory Text Analytics) | Grade: A
Description: Application of unsupervised machine learning methods to the linguistic, cultural, and sociological analysis of long-form textual sources. Students engage with digital humanities theory and methods, large digitized text libraries, structured data, relational databases, and linked data. Covers the full pipeline from raw text to interpretable models.
Key topics: Text preprocessing, tokenization, TF-IDF, word embeddings, topic modeling (LDA), vector space models, clustering, distributional semantics, sentiment analysis, document classification, NLP pipelines.

DS 6002 | Ethics of Big Data I | Grade: A+
Description: Examines ethical issues arising around big data and provides frameworks, context, concepts, and theories to help students think through and deal with these issues in professional settings. Covers fairness, accountability, transparency, digital rights, harm, bias, discrimination, and regulatory frameworks.
Key topics: AI ethics, fairness and bias in ML systems, data governance, regulatory frameworks, digital rights, responsible AI, technical debt, data justice, international and multistakeholder agreements.

DS 6015 | Data Science Capstone | Grade: A
Description: Capstone project course where interdisciplinary teams design, implement, and present an applied data science project for a real client. Teams meet with advisors and clients to advance work. Integrates all skills from the MSDS program into a complete research-to-deployment pipeline.
Key topics: End-to-end data science project management, client communication, applied ML/AI, team collaboration, presentation of technical results to non-technical stakeholders.
