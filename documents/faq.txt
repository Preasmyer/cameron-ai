Q: What is ABMIL and why did Cameron use it?
A: ABMIL stands for Attention-Based Multiple Instance Learning. Cameron used it because his melanoma research involved gigapixel whole-slide images that are far too large to pass through a standard CNN or ViT directly. ABMIL solves this by decomposing each slide into tiles, extracting embeddings from each tile using a pretrained feature extractor, then using a gated attention mechanism to learn which tiles are diagnostically relevant and aggregate them into a single slide-level prediction. This expanded image information retention from 0.0625% to 100% compared to downscaling approaches.

Q: How does Cameron's computer vision experience relate to LLMs?
A: The architectures are conceptually identical. Image patch decomposition maps to tokenization. DINOv2 and DenseNet tile embeddings map to token embedding lookup tables. ABMIL's gated attention (tanh times sigmoid) maps to multi-head self-attention with query, key, and value matrices. Frozen encoder to selective unfreezing is the exact same fine-tuning strategy used in LLMs. Catastrophic forgetting is a risk in both domains. Monte Carlo Cross-Validation rigor maps to LLM evaluation methodology.
